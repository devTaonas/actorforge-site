USPTO Trademark Search: Bulk-Check If a Phrase Is Trademarked

Paste up to 10,000 phrases and get a verdict for each (registered-live, pending, dead-only or clear) with the matching marks, owners, classes and TSDR links.

What it does

If you sell print-on-demand T-shirts, mugs or stickers, using a slogan someone else has registered is a risk you want to catch before upload. Checking phrases one at a time in USPTO Trademark Search works, but not when you have a few hundred designs in the queue.

This actor checks each phrase against the official US trademark register (the same database behind USPTO Trademark Search) and returns one row per phrase with a clear verdict: registered-live, pending, dead-only or clear, plus live, registered, pending and dead match counts. Each row lists the matching marks with word mark, serial and registration number, status, filing and registration dates, owner name with state and country, attorney, international classes, goods and services, mark type and a direct TSDR link. Choose exact, contains or words matching and narrow by Nice class, for example 25 for clothing or 21 for mugs.

It is built for Merch by Amazon, Etsy and Redbubble sellers, brand namers and agencies screening many phrases at once. It is not legal advice. A clear result only means no matching mark text was on the USPTO register at check time; it does not cover common-law use, state or foreign registrations, design-only marks or similar-but-not-identical marks. Talk to a trademark attorney before relying on a result. The actor is not affiliated with the USPTO.

How it works

  1. You paste phrases (case and extra spaces do not matter, duplicates are removed) and choose a match mode and optional international classes.
  2. Each phrase is searched against the public backend of USPTO Trademark Search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) at no more than 2 requests per second, with back-off on rate limits.
  3. All matching marks are counted by status to produce the verdict; the class filter, if set, applies to the verdict as well.
  4. Up to maxResultsPerPhrase marks are listed per phrase (live first, then registered, newest first), filtered by statusFilter.
  5. Invalid phrases, duplicates and failed checks are never charged, and the run stops cleanly at your spend limit.

Limits and honest notes

  • Not legal advice. A clear verdict means no matching mark text was found on the USPTO register at check time. It does not cover common-law (unregistered) use, state or foreign registrations, design-only marks or confusingly similar marks (e.g. different spelling). Consult a trademark attorney before relying on it.
  • Match modes: exact = the whole mark text equals the phrase (case-insensitive, a trailing . or ! is ignored); contains = the phrase appears anywhere inside the mark text, also inside longer words (CAT LOVER finds CRAZY CAT LOVERS); words = every word of the phrase appears in the mark, any order, with very common words such as IT or THE ignored by the USPTO index. When unsure, run contains, which is the most cautious.
  • The verdict and the live/registered/pending/dead counts always cover every status; statusFilter only changes which marks are listed in matches. pending = live application not (yet) registered; it can still block you.
  • The international class filter applies to the verdict too: with classes 25 and 21 a phrase registered only for software (class 9) comes back clear for your products.
  • Privacy: only the owner's name, entity type and state/country are returned (as published on the public register). Owner street addresses, emails and phone numbers are never returned; for attorneys only the name.
  • Gentle on USPTO: at most 2 requests per second with back-off on rate limits, so about 7,000 phrases per hour. For lists over ~6,000 phrases raise the run timeout above the default 1 hour.
  • Data comes from the public backend of USPTO Trademark Search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) and is as fresh as that service, usually updated daily. US government data is in the public domain.
  • Respects your run budget: set a maximum total charge and the run checks only as many phrases as it covers, stopping cleanly with every delivered row charged exactly once.

FAQ

How do I check if a phrase is trademarked in the US?

Add the phrase to phrases and run. The verdict tells you whether a live registered mark exists (registered-live), a live application is pending, only dead marks exist (dead-only) or nothing matches (clear), based on the USPTO register at the moment of the check.

Does a 'clear' result mean I can use the phrase?

No. Clear means no matching mark text was found on the USPTO register. It does not cover unregistered common-law rights, state or foreign registrations, design-only marks or confusingly similar marks such as different spellings. This is not legal advice; consult a trademark attorney before relying on it.

What is the difference between exact, contains and words matching?

exact matches marks whose whole text equals the phrase (a trailing . or ! is ignored). contains finds the phrase anywhere inside a mark, so CAT LOVER also finds CRAZY CAT LOVERS. words requires every word to appear in any order. When unsure, use contains, which is the most cautious.

Which trademark classes should I use for T-shirts and mugs?

Class 25 covers clothing and class 21 covers mugs and housewares; 16 is paper goods, stickers and posters, 18 bags and 9 phone cases and software. The class filter also changes the verdict, so a phrase registered only in class 9 comes back clear for classes 25 and 21.

Can a pending application still block me?

Yes. Pending means a live application is in examination and not yet registered, and it can still block you. The verdict and counts always cover every status, whatever you choose in statusFilter.

How much does it cost to check 1,000 phrases?

$30.00, at $0.03 per phrase checked, with the verdict, counts and up to 100 matching marks included. maxResultsPerPhrase does not change the price, and invalid phrases, duplicates and failed checks are free.
