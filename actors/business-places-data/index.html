Google Maps Scraper Alternative: Local Business Leads from Open Data

Type a city and a business type, get one clean row per business with contacts, coordinates and licence, from 80M+ open places worldwide.

Run on Apify →See pricing

What it does

Most local lead lists start with a Google Maps scraper, and most of the pain comes with it: blocked requests, proxies, CAPTCHAs and a results list that stops at around 120 places per search. If what you actually need is names, addresses, phone numbers and websites for every dentist in Austin or every restaurant in Berlin, you do not need Google at all.

Business Places Data reads the Overture Maps Foundation places dataset, an open GeoParquet release of 80M+ business points of interest refreshed monthly and built from Meta, Microsoft, Foursquare, AllThePlaces, BrightQuery and other sources. Nothing is scraped, so a whole city comes back in seconds. Each row carries the business name, category, full address, latitude/longitude, E.164 phone numbers, website, business email, social links and brand, plus a plain Google Maps search link.

It is built for agencies, SDR teams and data people who want permissive, resellable lead data. Every row lists its sources, licence and attribution, and the licences (CDLA-Permissive-2.0, Apache-2.0, CC0) are not share-alike, so you may store, enrich and resell the leads as long as attribution travels with them. The trade-off is honest: this is not Google data, so there are no reviews, star ratings, opening hours or photos.

How it works

  1. You give a city name (32,000+ cities are built in), a bounding box, or a lat/lon centre with a radius, plus optional categories such as dentist or plumber.
  2. The actor finds the newest monthly Overture release and reads only the GeoParquet row groups that overlap your area, typically 15-45 MB for a city.
  3. Places are filtered by category (2,100+ Overture ids, with plain-word synonyms), name, confidence and whether they have a website, phone or email.
  4. Phones are normalised to E.164, possibly personal emails are removed, and each row is written with its sources, licence and attribution.
  5. Results are ordered by confidence (or by distance for a radius search) and the run stops at your maxPlaces cap or spend limit.

Limits and honest notes

  • Open data, not Google: no reviews, ratings, review counts, opening hours, photos, popular times or price levels. The Google Maps link in each row is a plain search URL built from the name and address; nothing is fetched from Google.
  • Freshness follows Overture's monthly releases. The newest release is found automatically (or pin one with release). operatingStatus is often empty, so a closed business can still appear until a source drops it; minConfidence (default 0.5) filters most stale records.
  • Coverage varies by country and category. In the test cities almost every dentist and restaurant had a phone and a website; emails were present for about 75% of Austin dentists but about 32% of Berlin restaurants. Rural areas and parts of Africa and Asia are thinner.
  • Categories are Overture's taxonomy (2,100+ ids such as dental_clinic or italian_restaurant). Common plain words are mapped for you; run listCategories (free) to browse the rest.
  • One query per run, up to 1.5 x 1.5 degrees (about one metro area). For a region or country, run once per city. A city query typically reads 15-45 MB of public GeoParquet in 5-15 seconds at 1 GB memory.
  • Business contacts only: phones, emails and socials are the business's published contacts. Free-mail addresses that do not contain the business name and off-domain personal-looking addresses are removed (counted in droppedPersonalEmails). You remain responsible for how you use the data under GDPR, CAN-SPAM and similar laws.
  • City names resolve offline from a built-in list of 32,000+ cities (Overture divisions, OpenStreetMap-based); smaller towns are looked up in Overture's divisions data and need a region or country ("Dripping Springs, TX"). No external geocoder is called.
  • The run's spend limit is respected: the area query is reserved first, places are charged before delivery, and the run stops cleanly at the limit.

FAQ

Is this Google Maps data?

No. Rows come from the Overture Maps Foundation places dataset, which is built from Meta, Microsoft, Foursquare, AllThePlaces, BrightQuery and other sources. The Google Maps link in each row is a plain search URL built from the name and address; nothing is fetched from Google.

How many businesses can I get from one city?

One run covers one area of up to 1.5 x 1.5 degrees, roughly a metro area, and returns up to 20,000 places (maxPlaces). There is no 120-result cap. For a region or country, run once per city.

How often is the business data updated?

Freshness follows Overture's monthly releases, and the newest release is picked automatically. You can pin an older one with the release field. A closed business can still appear until a source drops it; the default minConfidence of 0.5 filters most stale records.

Does it return business email addresses?

Yes, where the source data has them. Emails on the business's own domain and role mailboxes such as info@ are kept; free-mail addresses are kept only when they contain the business name, and others are dropped and counted in droppedPersonalEmails. In testing, about 75% of Austin dentists had an email but only about 32% of Berlin restaurants.

Can I use the leads commercially?

The source licences (CDLA-Permissive-2.0, Apache-2.0, CC0) allow commercial use, storage and redistribution as long as the attribution and licence texts travel with the rows. You remain responsible for how you contact people under GDPR, CAN-SPAM and similar laws.

What does a typical run cost?

$0.003 per area query plus $0.001 per place delivered. 200 dentists in Austin cost $0.203; 1,000 restaurants in Berlin cost $1.003. Places filtered out or beyond your cap are never charged.
