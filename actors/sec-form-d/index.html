SEC Form D Scraper — Startup Funding Leads & Private Placements

Every SEC Form D and D/A filing from EDGAR as one row: issuer, industry, state, incorporation year, 506(b)/506(c)/504 exemptions, offering size, amount raised, investors, minimum investment. Filter by date, state, industry, amount or name. $10 per 1,000 filings.

What it does

Every startup funding round and private placement filed with the SEC, as clean rows. When a US company or fund raises money privately under Regulation D, it must file a Form D with the SEC within 15 days of the first sale. This actor reads every new Form D and D/A from EDGAR's daily indexes and parses the filing's XML into one row: issuer name, CIK, entity type, state and year of incorporation, business city/state, industry group and sector, revenue or NAV range, the federal exemptions claimed (Rule 506(b), 506(c), 504, Section 4(a)(5), Investment Company Act 3(c)(1)/3(c)(7)), date of first sale, total offering amount, amount sold, amount remaining and percent sold, number of investors, minimum investment, sales commissions and finder's fees, securities types (equity, debt, fund interests), placement agents with CRD numbers, the previous accession number for amendments, and EDGAR URLs. Filter by filing date, state, industry, exemption, minimum amount sold or offering size, issuer name or CIK. Use it to find startups that just raised for sales prospecting and lead generation, track VC, PE, hedge fund and SPV launches, monitor competitors' rounds, build deal-flow and fundraising datasets, or research private-market activity by state and sector. Executives, directors and promoters are off by default (opt in for name, role, city and state only). Simple pricing: $10 per 1,000 filings, no compute bill on top; filtered-out filings are never charged.

Limits and honest notes

  • Source: SEC EDGAR daily form indexes and each filing's primary_doc.xml. EDGAR publishes a business day's index at about 10 pm US Eastern, so filings appear the next morning (not in real time). Weekends and federal holidays have no index.
  • Form D XML exists from 2009 onward; dateFrom is clamped to 2009-01-01. At most 366 days per run — split longer backfills into several runs. Results are newest filing date first.
  • SEC fair-access rules: requests are paced to 5 per second, so a run takes roughly 1 minute per 250 filings parsed. Filters on issuer name, CIK and amendments are applied before downloading, so they are fastest; state, industry, exemption and amount filters need each filing's XML.
  • 'Indefinite' offering amounts (common for funds) are returned as totalOfferingAmount: null with offeringAmountIndefinite: true, and always pass the minimum offering amount filter.
  • Amounts are as reported by the issuer, in US dollars. Form D is a notice filed within 15 days of the first sale, not a final close: totalAmountSold is the amount sold so far, and later D/A amendments update it.
  • Privacy: related persons (executive officers, directors, promoters) and the signer are public SEC records but personal data, so they are excluded unless includeRelatedPersons is on — and then only name, relationship, city and state, never street addresses. The issuer's business address and phone are always included.
  • Respects your run budget: set a maximum total charge and the run stops cleanly at the cap, with every delivered row charged exactly once.

  }

Python

# pip install apify-client   (set APIFY_TOKEN in your environment)
import os
from apify_client import ApifyClient

client = ApifyClient(os.environ["APIFY_TOKEN"])
run_input = {
    "maxResults": 50
}

run = client.actor("actor-forge/sec-form-d").call(run_input=run_input)
for item in client.dataset(run["defaultDatasetId"]).iterate_items():
    print(item)

The run-sync-get-dataset-items endpoint waits for the run (up to 5 minutes) and returns the dataset rows directly. For longer runs, start the run asynchronously and read the dataset when it finishes, as the Python example does. Replace APIFY_TOKEN with your own token from the Apify console.
