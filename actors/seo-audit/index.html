SEO Audit Crawler: On-Page and Technical SEO Check for Every Page

A cloud site audit that crawls like a search engine and gives every page a list of issues, a severity for each and a 0-100 score.

What it does

Spot-checking a few pages misses the problems that only show up across a site: duplicate titles, pages nothing links to, internal links that hit 404s or redirect chains. The SEO Audit Crawler follows your links and your sitemap, respects robots.txt and runs more than 40 technical and on-page checks on every HTML page it fetches.

Each page gets HTTP status and redirect chain, indexability (meta robots, X-Robots-Tag, canonical), title and meta description checks including duplicates, H1 and heading outline, word count and thin content, images missing alt text, link counts, broken internal links, inlinks, hreflang, Open Graph and Twitter cards, schema.org JSON-LD, mixed content and URL hygiene. Issues are graded error, warning or notice, and the page score starts at 100 and drops 10, 5 or 1 point per distinct issue.

A site summary adds average score, top issues by pages affected, duplicate title and description groups, the most-linked broken URLs and orphan pages that are in your sitemap but linked from nowhere. It costs $6 per 1,000 pages audited ($0.60 for a 100-page site), and pages that answer an error are reported free. It suits SEOs, agencies and developers who want a scheduled audit without installing desktop software.

How it works

  1. You give one or more startUrls; the crawl stays on that site and under each URL's path unless you set includeGlobs.
  2. It follows links up to maxDepth clicks and, with useSitemap on, reads sitemap.xml and sitemaps listed in robots.txt, then audits sitemap URLs no page links to as orphan pages.
  3. Pages are fetched as static HTML, at most 3 requests at a time, pausing on HTTP 429 for the Retry-After period.
  4. Once the crawl finishes it computes the cross-page checks (duplicates, broken internal links, inlinks, hreflang return links) and writes one row per page.
  5. The site-level summary is saved to the run's key-value store as the OUTPUT record.

Use cases

Pre-launch or post-migration check

Audit a staging or freshly migrated site to catch missing titles, noindex tags left on by mistake, canonicals pointing elsewhere and redirect chains before search engines find them.

Find orphan pages in your sitemap

With useSitemap on, sitemap URLs that no crawled page links to are audited and flagged orphan-page. Raise maxPages to cover the whole site so the result is conclusive. The sitemap audit guide walks through it.

Weekly score tracking

Schedule a weekly run and compare the average score and top issues in the summary. A sudden jump in errors usually points to a template change or a deploy that broke something.

Audit one section only

Start at https://example.com/blog to audit just the blog, or use includeGlobs and excludeGlobs to target docs and skip tag and pagination archives.

Limits and honest notes

  • Pages are fetched as static HTML without a browser: fast and cheap, and it covers almost all websites. Content, links and tags that exist only after JavaScript runs (single-page apps that render client-side) are not seen, which is also how many search engine and AI crawlers see your site. Core Web Vitals and other browser performance metrics are not measured.
  • Thresholds: titles 30-60 characters, meta descriptions 70-160 characters, thin content under 200 words (counted in the page body without navigation, header, footer, sidebars and scripts), URLs over 115 characters, responses slower than 2 seconds.
  • The score starts at 100 and loses 10 points per distinct error, 5 per warning and 1 per notice. Duplicate titles and descriptions are compared across indexable pages only (noindex and canonicalised copies are expected to repeat), case and spacing aside.
  • Broken internal links and links to redirects are found from the pages the crawl fetched; internal links beyond your page limit, and external links, are not checked (use a broken link checker for that). Orphan pages are sitemap URLs no audited page links to, so they are only conclusive when the crawl covered the whole site (orphanCheckComplete in the summary).
  • Crawling respects robots.txt and stays on your site and under the start URL's path, unless you set include globs. Pages behind a login are not supported. Polite by design: at most 3 requests at a time to your site, and a 429 'Too Many Requests' pauses it for its Retry-After. Pages refused by bot protection are reported as 'fetch-blocked' rows, free.
  • Rows are delivered when the crawl finishes, since duplicates, broken links, hreflang return links and inlinks need every page; runs resume after a platform migration without re-charging work already done. Pages larger than 5 MB and non-HTML files are skipped.
  • Links to internal network addresses (localhost, private IPs, cloud metadata) are never requested and never charged.
  • Respects your run budget: set a maximum total charge and the run stops cleanly at the cap and delivers every page audited so far.

FAQ

How do I audit every page of my website for SEO?

Put your homepage in startUrls and set maxPages above your site's page count (it defaults to 200). Leave useSitemap on so pages only reachable via the sitemap are included too. Each page becomes one row with its issues and score.

What counts as a title that is too long or a thin page?

Titles are checked against 30-60 characters and meta descriptions against 70-160. Thin content means under 200 words in the page body, excluding navigation, header, footer, sidebars and scripts. URLs over 115 characters and responses slower than 2 seconds are also flagged.

Does it render JavaScript?

No. Pages are fetched as static HTML, which is fast and cheap but means content that only appears after JavaScript runs is not seen. That is also how many search engine and AI crawlers first see a site.

Are pages that return errors charged?

No. Pages that answer 4xx/5xx, time out or are refused by bot protection are reported as rows free of charge. Only HTML pages fetched and audited are charged, at $0.006 each.

Where is the site-wide summary?

It is saved in the run's default key-value store as the OUTPUT record. It lists pages audited, average score, top issues, duplicate groups, broken internal links, orphan sitemap pages and whether robots.txt and a sitemap exist.
