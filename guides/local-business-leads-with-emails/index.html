How to Build a Local Business Lead List With Emails (Without Scraping Google Maps)

Uses Google Maps Email Extractor Alternative — Business Leads

Short answer: Type a city and a business type into Business Leads with Emails. It pulls matching places from the open Overture Maps dataset, reads each business's own website (home page plus up to two contact pages) for company emails, phones and socials, and returns one lead row per business with the source of every email.

The usual way to build a local lead list is to scrape Google Maps and then crawl each listing's website for an email address. It works until it does not: Google Maps scraping depends on proxies and browser automation, and Google's terms do not allow it.

Business Leads with Emails gets the same kind of list from two sources that are open about being used this way. Places come from the Overture Maps Foundation places dataset (more than 80 million businesses worldwide, refreshed monthly, under permissive licences). Contacts come from each business's own website, read once, with robots.txt obeyed. Every email in the output says where it was found.

This guide covers defining the area and category, deciding how much website reading you need, filtering to leads with emails, and what coverage to expect. If you only need names, addresses and phones, the cheaper Business Places Data actor and its guide are enough.

Step by step

  1. Describe the area

    Put a city in location: "Austin, TX", "Berlin", "London, Ontario". About 32,000 cities with 15,000+ people are built in; smaller towns need a region or country. You can also type the whole query, "dentists in Austin TX", and the part before "in" becomes the category. For precise areas, use lat, lon and radiusKm (results then come nearest first) or a bbox of up to 1.5 × 1.5 degrees. Add countryCode when a city name exists in several countries.

  2. Pick categories

    Use plain words in categories (dentist, plumber, restaurant, lawyer, hotel) or Overture category ids (dental_clinic, italian_restaurant). A parent category includes its children. To see all 2,100+ ids with worldwide counts, run once with listCategories on and a filter such as "dent"; that run is free. Leave categories empty to get every place in the area, which is rarely what you want for outreach.

  3. Decide how deep to read websites

    enrichWebsites is on by default. Each business's website is read once: the home page plus up to maxPagesPerSite − 1 contact, imprint or about pages. The website charge is per website, not per page, so keep maxPagesPerSite at 3 unless speed matters. includeWebsiteSignals adds facts from the home page already downloaded (HTTPS, CMS or site builder, shop platform, analytics and ad pixels, booking widget, copyright year), which are useful if you sell web or marketing services.

  4. Filter to the leads you will actually use

    Turn on onlyWithEmail to deliver only businesses that ended up with a company email; the rest are dropped and never charged. requirePhone does the same for phone numbers. websiteFilter set to with keeps only businesses with their own website; without is for agencies looking for businesses that have none. maxPlaces (200 by default) is applied before any website is read, so it also caps cost.

    {
  "location": "Austin, TX",
  "categories": ["dentist"],
  "maxPlaces": 300,
  "enrichWebsites": true,
  "maxPagesPerSite": 3,
  "onlyWithEmail": true,
  "includeWebsiteSignals": true,
  "minConfidence": 0.5
}

  5. Export and check the sources

    Download the dataset as CSV or Excel. Before outreach, look at emailSources (Overture, or the exact page of the business's site where the address was found) and websiteStatus. A status of blocked means the site refused automated readers, so the row carries Overture's contacts only. Keep the attribution field if you republish the data; it carries the credit lines the licences require.

Tips and pitfalls

  • Email coverage varies by trade and country. On 2026-10-22, 50 Austin dentists gave 68% with a company email from Overture alone and 74% after reading the websites; 50 Berlin restaurants gave 0% and 52%. Test a small area first.
  • Raise minConfidence to 0.9 for cleaner lists in dense cities, or lower it towards 0 for rural areas with fewer, older records.
  • Use keyword to find a brand or a word in the business name, such as "orthodontics" or "Starbucks".
  • Sites that hide emails in images or behind JavaScript forms will not yield an address. contactFormDetected tells you where a form is the only route.

Limitations

  • This is not Google data: there are no reviews, star ratings, review counts, opening hours, photos or price levels.
  • Places are as fresh as the latest monthly Overture release, and coverage differs by country.
  • Websites behind strict bot protection (often 20 to 35% of a city's sites) come back as blocked. The actor uses no proxies or browser and stops at the first 403, 429 or CAPTCHA.
  • One area per run, up to about one metro area and 5,000 places.

Responsible use

Only company mailboxes are kept, by allowlist: role addresses such as info@, kontakt@, office@ or bookings@, and addresses made of the business name or domain. Personal-looking addresses (first names, surnames, initials, owner addresses from an imprint) are dropped and counted, not delivered.

Cold email is regulated. Before contacting anyone, check what GDPR, PECR, CAN-SPAM, CASL or your local rules require for business-to-business outreach, give a clear way to opt out, and honour it. You are responsible for how you use the list.

FAQ

Is this the same data as Google Maps?

No. Places come from the Overture Maps Foundation, which merges open and contributed datasets. Many businesses appear in both, but the fields differ, and there are no Google reviews, ratings or opening hours.

Can I use the leads commercially?

Overture's place data is under non-share-alike licences (CDLA Permissive 2.0, Apache 2.0, CC0) that allow commercial use with attribution, which each row carries. Contacting the businesses is subject to marketing and privacy law in their country.

Why did a business with a website get no email?

Check websiteStatus. The site may have blocked automated readers, disallowed it in robots.txt, shown the address only as an image, or published only a personal address, which is dropped.

Which countries work?

Any country Overture covers, which is most of the world. Density is highest in North America and Western Europe. Try a small maxPlaces run to see coverage for your area.
